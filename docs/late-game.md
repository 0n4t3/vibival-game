# Late game: boats and metals

[Guide home](README.md)

> **Late game.** Everything on this page is late-game content, built on the
> `late-game` branch. It is not in the main game until that branch is merged.
> The design notes behind it are in [boats.md](../boats.md) and
> [metals.md](../metals.md).

Once you have a house, a workbench and a steady supply of boards, the swamp
opens up: build a dock and a raft, pole out to the rocky islands far
offshore, and bring back copper. Later, iron falls from the sky.

## Docks

A **Dock Blueprint** is drawn at a workbench on a sheet of paper with the ash
pencil and a blade to sharpen it, like a house blueprint. Hold it at the water's edge, facing out over
open water: an outline shows where the dock would go. It is forgiving: if the
spot right in front of you won't do, it slides along the bank and turns a
little to find one that will. Right-click to mark out the site.

The dock is built part by part, like a house. Look at the glowing part and
kneel (C) with what it needs laid out:

| Part | Needs |
|---|---|
| Land post | A Calamites log (carried), pounded in with a river stone |
| Four piles | A Calamites log each, pounded in standing in the water |
| Spikes | 16 sticks and 8 cordage, and a blade to sharpen them |
| Stringers | 4 Calamites boards and 4 cordage |
| Deck | 12 tree fern boards and 6 cordage |
| Slipway | 2 branches, 4 Calamites boards and 2 cordage |
| Mooring post | A branch and 2 cordage |

**A finished dock is safe.** No big amphibian comes for you on it, in the
water within 7 m of its sides and end, or on the bank up to 30 m behind it,
so stepping off it onto land is safe too: they won't lie among the spiked
piles. Until it is
finished it protects nothing. Before it is finished, three blows of an axe or
mattock tear it down and leave what it took on the ground.

## Rafts

A finished dock marks out a raft on its slipway. Build it the same way: six
Calamites logs laid side by side, 3 Calamites boards across them, the logs
lashed together (8 cordage and 4 string), a mooring line (2 cordage), and
then launch it.

**Poling.** Split a stripped Calamites log into 2 **Calamites Poles** (kneel
at it, C). Step onto the raft and press E to board. Holding a pole, the
movement keys push the raft along, at any depth. It is slow, keeps drifting
once it is moving, and the wind pushes it. It rides very high, so it floats
in all but the barest shallows; where it grounds, step off and wade. Step off
next to a finished dock and it ties up there. It can't be poled in under a
dock: it bumps off the piles.

| Pole | How | Jab |
|---|---|---|
| Calamites Pole | Split from a stripped Calamites log | Weak, long reach |
| Fire-Hardened Pole | Char a pole's tip in a campfire | A little harder |
| Copper-Shod Pole | A pole and a copper pole shoe, by hand | Hard |
| Iron-Shod Pole | A pole and an iron pole shoe, by hand | The best |

**Cargo.** A raft has no hold. Sacks set down on it (Use) are lashed to its
four corners and ride with it, and one hauled thing (a log, a pot, an anvil
stone, a meteorite) rides in the middle. Weight slows it and makes it ride
lower, and a load packed to one side makes it list.

**Bites.** Out on deep, open water a big amphibian may clamp its jaws on a
corner. You'll see ripples first, then the corner dips. You have a few
seconds to strike the biter with anything (a jab of the pole will do) before
it drags the corner under and tips you in, then one last, shorter chance.
Bites never damage the raft, never happen within a finished dock's or an
island's safe water, and never come twice within 20 seconds. Night is
riskier than day.

## The rocky islands

Five rocky islands stand far out in the open water, 600 to 740 m from where
you start (about 140 to 280 m out past the edge of the marsh). No water predator comes within 25 m of an island's shelf, so the
island and the water round it are safe. On them you find:

- **Copper outcrops**: humps of rock streaked green. A copper hammer breaks
  out 3 to 5 lumps of **Copper Ore**; then it takes days before more is
  worth breaking loose.
- **Anvil stones**: lumpy basalt boulders, too heavy for the bag. Carry one
  in your arms (and home on the raft) and set it down where you want an
  anvil, then shape it (see *Shaping an anvil*). Peck it flat first and it
  is lighter to carry home.
- **Native copper** in the gravel along their shores: lumps of rock threaded
  with bright copper, washed up with the stones. Every island always has
  some: four lumps wash up at its waterline when you come near, and lie
  there for one to three days. Those you pick up are replaced only once the
  rest wash away and a fresh batch comes in.
- The **copper hammer** is a short-handled tool with a square cast head.

## Copper

### Charcoal

A **Charcoal Clamp** is 10 firewood and 4 clay, built by hand where you
kneel (you need a mattock). Light it with an ember or a lit torch (E). It
smoulders for a day, then a mattock breaks it open for 8 to 12 **Charcoal**.
Opened early, some of the wood is still only charred sticks. Charcoal burns
hot and long: the furnace needs it, and in a campfire it outlasts wood.

### The clay furnace

Lay a ring of 8 river stones by hand (you need a mattock). The rest goes up
part by part: a coiled clay shaft (12 clay and 2 ash), the tuyere (2 clay
at a bench with the clay platter, fired in a campfire), a first firing to
harden it (4 firewood), and two blowpipes (a Calamites shoot and a clay tip at a bench with the woodworking
tools, fired). Your first finished furnace opens the metal recipes in the
book.

All hot work is done kneeling at the furnace (C). Every click while casting
or smelting is a breath down the blowpipes: it raises the heat, shown by the
glow (dull red, cherry, orange). Copper only melts at full heat (orange).
Left alone the furnace cools, faster in rain.

### Getting copper

- **Native copper**: smash it on an anvil with a river stone, then kneel at
  the water with a **Washing Pan** (4 clay and 1 ash at a bench with the clay
  platter, fired) and wash the grit away: **Copper Grains**, ready for a
  mould.
- **Copper ore**: crush it on an anvil with a copper hammer (a river stone
  won't do), wash two crushed ores into one **Sifted Copper Ore**, then smelt
  two sifted ores with 2 charcoal at the furnace: **Unrefined Copper**.

Your first copper has to come from native copper, since crushing ore needs a
copper hammer.

### Moulds and casting

Moulds are 3 clay and 1 ash with a blade, at a bench with the clay platter,
then fired. Each cracks after 5 castings. Lay a mould at the furnace with
copper and a charcoal and blow until it is cast:

| Mould | Copper | Casts |
|---|---|---|
| Axe | 3 | Rough copper axe head |
| Knife | 2 | Rough copper knife blade |
| Spear | 2 | Rough copper spear head |
| Hammer | 2 | Copper hammer head |
| Saw | 3 | Rough copper saw blade |
| Bar | 1 | Copper bar (for a chisel or a pole shoe) |
| Hook | 1 | 3 copper fishhooks |
| Beads | 1 | A string of copper beads (a trophy) |

### Shaping an anvil

An anvil stone starts as a rough boulder. Kneel at it (C) and work it in
three stages, each where it lies:

1. **Peck the face flat** with a river stone: click the lumps on its face
   to knock them down a level at a time until it is flat. A blow on stone
   that is already flat is only a wasted blow, and you can stop and come
   back later. That makes a **rough anvil**, 12 kg lighter. Smashing native
   copper, crushing ore and crushing stone go quicker on it.
2. **Grind the face smooth** with 2 **Stone Grit** (two river stones crushed
   together give 2 grit and you keep one stone), a pot of water and a river
   stone. You get the pot back empty. That makes a **dressed anvil**, where
   castings, bars and iron can be worked.
3. **Peck a whetstone seat** into its corner with a river stone. Then a
   whetstone can be seated in it.

### The anvil and the whetstone

A **Whetstone** is two river stones ground together with a pot of water (you
keep one stone). Hold it and press E at a finished anvil to seat it in the
anvil's corner (E again takes it back). Then kneel at the anvil (C) to grind
rough castings sharp, grind a copper bar into a chisel or a pole shoe, and
sharpen blunt metal tools. Haft a sharp head by hand with a stick and
cordage (a spear takes 2 sticks). A copper axe head hafted crosswise makes a
copper mattock.

### Copper tools

| Tool | What it does better |
|---|---|
| Copper Axe | Fells every tree, the hard Lepidodendron and Sigillaria too (slowly) |
| Copper Knife | Butchers faster than chert |
| Copper Spear | Long reach and a heavy point |
| Copper Mattock | Digs faster than stone |
| Copper Saw | Saws any log straight into boards, hardwoods too |
| Copper Chisel | Bites into meteoric iron |
| Copper Hammer | Breaks out and crushes ore, works iron cold |
| Copper fishhook | Rig a rod with it at a bench, or swap it onto a rod by hand. Every bite is a fish, and the bait stays on until you land one |

### Copper containers

Both are made at the anvil with a copper hammer.

**Copper flask** (2 copper bars, 1 wax, 1 cordage): a flat, round flask
with a wooden stopper on a cord. It works like the wooden canteen, with more
room and less weight:

| | Wooden canteen | Copper flask |
|---|---|---|
| Water or soup | 2 servings | 4 servings |
| Alcohol | 10 measures | 20 measures |
| Weight, empty | 1.2 kg | 1.4 kg (two canteens: 2.4 kg) |
| Weight, full of water | 4.2 kg | 7.4 kg (two canteens: 8.4 kg) |
| Soup keeps | as long as in the pot | twice as long |

- Fill it at the water (four servings of swamp water at once), or a serving
  at a time from a soup pot.
- Each serving of soup you add mixes into what is already there, weighted by
  the number of servings.
- Each draw from a sealed vessel adds 10 measures of alcohol (one serving of
  the brew) until the flask holds 20. A canteen still tops up to 10.
- A flask of boiled water pours into a fermentation vessel a serving at a
  time, like a canteen.

**Copper soup pot** (5 copper bars, 3 boards, 1 cordage): it is left beside
the anvil, and needs no firing. Compared with the clay soup pot:

- It holds 20 servings instead of 16, and 15 food instead of 12, so a
  serving is just as rich.
- It comes to the boil in 40 seconds instead of 60.
- Its soup keeps 20% longer after it last boiled: about 43 game hours
  instead of 36.

Soup scooped from a copper pot keeps for whatever part of its life it has
left, in whatever container it goes into.

## Tool wear

- **Stone tools** (the stone axe, chert spear and stone mattock) are safe for
  their first 50 uses. After that, each use has a small chance (2%) to snap
  the head off, leaving the haft in your hand.
- **Metal tools** go blunt: a copper edge lasts 30 uses, an iron edge 45.
  Blunt, a tool does half damage and jobs take twice as many steps until you
  sharpen it at the anvil's whetstone.
- **The copper hammer** wears out working iron: its face mushrooms a little
  with every strike (60 in all). Worn out, it gives back 2 copper grains to
  melt down. An iron hammer never wears out.

A use is a hit that lands, a finished job that needed the tool, or a dig. A
job uses the tool in your hand if it will do, otherwise the best one you
carry (iron over copper over stone, sharp over blunt). Part-worn tools keep
their own slots.

## Meteoric iron

**Meteorites fall in meteor showers.** About half of all showers, and always
the first after you have cast copper, bring one down on dry land 60 to 300 m
away, never within 60 m of anything you have built or set down. If you are
awake you see the fireball streak that way and are told how far off it hit
and in which direction; a few seconds later the impact slams through the
ground and shakes you. From day 10 there are daytime meteor showers too:
fireballs streaking across the sky, trailing smoke. Those burn up high with
a distant double boom; one that comes down hits far harder, shakes the ground
under you and leaves a column of smoke.

A tall column of dark smoke rises from where it landed, high over the trees,
thinning away over the next day: walk towards it. The meteorite lies half
buried in a scorched, smoking crater. Three mattock
swings dig it out. Carry it home (on the raft if need be), or kneel at it
with a copper chisel and a copper hammer to chip lumps of **Meteoric Iron**
off it (an iron chisel is twice as fast). Chipping wears it down until it is
gone.

On an anvil, beat the iron clean with a copper hammer: a small lump makes
**Refined Iron** (for a knife blade, a chisel or a pole shoe), a large one a
**Refined Iron Bar** (for an axe, spear, mattock or hammer head). Hammer it
to shape, grind it sharp at the whetstone, and haft it.

| Tool | What it does better |
|---|---|
| Iron Axe | Fells every tree as fast as a soft one; holds its edge longer |
| Iron Knife | The best blade there is |
| Iron Spear | The best weapon there is |
| Iron Mattock | Digs fastest of all |
| Iron Chisel | Chips meteoric iron twice as fast as copper |
| Iron Hammer | Works faster than copper, and never wears out |

## Weather in the late game

Rare weather is more common: about one day in three instead of one in five.
From day 10 a daytime meteor shower joins the list, and any meteor shower
may bring a meteorite down (see above).
