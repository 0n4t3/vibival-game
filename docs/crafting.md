# Gathering and crafting

[Guide home](README.md)

## Gathering

Look at something and press E.

| Source | Gives | Regrows after |
|---|---|---|
| Fern (strip it): a big, bright green fern with curled fiddleheads in the middle and pale, stringy old fronds round its foot. The darker ferns all around are only scenery | 2–3 plant fiber, up to 2 fiddleheads. More fiber with a blade. Stripped, it is cut-back stalks until it grows back | 30–50 days |
| Tree fern (cut fronds) | 1–2 fronds, 1–2 plant fiber | 3 days |
| Seed fern (gather seeds) | 2–4 seeds, sometimes a frond | 5 days |
| Seed fern (dig it up: two mattock swings) | 4 Seed Fern Roots, plus its seeds if they hadn't been picked | Never: the plant is gone for good |
| Rotting log (search it) | 1–3 grubs, 1–2 bark tinder, up to 2 sticks | about 2 days |
| Clay bank (dig) | 2–4 wet clay, on low muddy banks | about 2 days |
| On the ground | Sticks everywhere; river stones and chert nodules, mostly on rocky ground | Picked-up items do not come back |
| Along the shore | River stones, chert nodules and sticks washed up at the water's edge, and salt crusts on the bank just above it (for salting food in a sealed fermentation vessel) | Each lies there 1–3 days; new ones keep washing up |

**Seed fern roots** are edible raw: a little greens, almost no food. Plant
them (see Farming below) to grow new seed ferns.

Plants grow back slowly: the exact wait is rolled each time you harvest, from
30 to 50 game days. Spread your gathering out and don't strip one grove bare.

Only some tree ferns can be cut. Those have a **fresh crown**: a tuft of bright
young fronds and curled fiddleheads standing up from the top of the trunk. The
crown is out of reach, so look at the foot of the trunk, from any side, and
press E. Once you cut one, its fresh crown is gone until it grows back, 3 game
days later.

Items you drop (Q, or **Drop** in the inventory) stay where you left them, and
are still there after you save and load. Food and anything else that spoils
still rots on the ground, just as it would in your bag. **Discard** in the inventory throws
an item away: it lies on the ground for now, but is gone once you reload.

### Piles

Raw materials you drop on dry ground go on a **pile** of their kind: stones,
chert, sticks, fiber, fronds, tinder, clay, wax, ash, chitin, cordage, bark,
branches, firewood, boards and logs. Drop another of the same thing within
about 1.5 m and it joins the pile. A pile holds one kind of thing, as many as
you like, though it only shows about a dozen. Look at a pile and press E to
take one off the top; hold E to keep taking. A log pile hands you a log to
carry. Food, containers and tools are dropped loose as before, and nothing is
piled in the water.

Kills leave carcasses. Small ones go into your bag; medium and large ones are
carried or dragged, and butchered where they lie with a blade: gutted,
skinned (the arthropods give chitin plates) and cut into raw meat. See
[Butchering](creatures.md#butchering).

## Woodcutting

You need a **Stone Axe**, in your bag or laid out.

1. **Fell a tree.** Walk up to a Calamites or a tree fern, face the trunk and
   press **C** to kneel at its foot. Choose *Fell*, then click the glowing
   notch again and again (5 to 10 strokes, more for a thicker trunk). The tree
   falls away from you in one piece and leaves a stump. Lepidodendron and
   Sigillaria are too hard for stone: they will need a metal axe.
2. **Cut it into logs.** Kneel (C) at the cut end of the trunk. Each *Cut off
   a log* takes three strokes and leaves a 2 m log lying beside the trunk; you
   shuffle along to the next cut. The last piece, with the crown, becomes a
   log or short log itself (a tree fern also gives a few fronds).
3. **Carry logs** by looking at one and pressing E. A log looks like the tree
   it came from: Calamites logs are green, ribbed and jointed, tree fern logs
   dark and shaggy with old frond bases, Lepidodendron logs covered in
   diamond-shaped scales, Sigillaria logs deeply ribbed. Stripped, each shows
   its own wood. Only logs from the same kind of tree pile together. Logs and short logs are
   held in your arms, not in the bag: they add no weight, but until you put
   them down (Q or E) you can't sprint, use or pick up anything, open your
   bag, craft or sleep. Put a log down beside another log of the same kind and
   they start a log pile.
4. **Work a log.** Kneel (C) beside a loose log:

   | Work | On | Strokes | Gives |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Strip the log | Log | 4 | Bark x6 and Branch x3 into your bag; the log becomes a Stripped Log |
   | Cut into short logs | Log or Stripped Log | 6 | Four Short Logs lying where it was |
   | Hew into boards | Stripped Log | 6 | Board x4, coloured by the tree's wood (any tree's boards will do in a recipe) |
   | Split into firewood | Short Log | 3 | Firewood x4 |

   A **Branch** snaps into three sticks: lay it out (C) and choose *Snap into
   sticks*. **Firewood** burns for 7 minutes, bark for one, a branch for
   almost four.

**Rot.** A felled tree left untouched goes rotten after about 3 days: it
turns dark, and you can search it like any rotting log for grubs, tinder and
sticks. About 3 days later it falls apart. Once you have cut into it, it
keeps. Logs keep on dry ground for good, but a log left lying in water soaks
through and rots in about 3 days, then falls apart.

**Stumps.** A new tree grows up out of the stump. You can watch it grow, and
after about 18 days (16 to 20) it is full grown and can be felled again. To
clear the spot for good, swing a **Mattock** at the stump: five blows dig it
out, roots and all, for a root of that tree (plant it to grow a new one) and
2 or 3 firewood.

## Farming

1. **Dig a hole.** Hold a **Mattock**, look at open ground close by and
   right-click (Use). Not in water or wet mud, on rocky ground, beside a tree
   or right next to another hole.
2. **Plant something in it.** Look at the hole and press C to kneel at it.
   Lay out a seed or a root (and Compost, if you like) and choose *Plant the
   seed* or *Plant the seed with compost*. Click the hole to set it in, again
   to put the compost round it, and again to fill the hole in over it. Any
   other item can be buried the same way (*Bury the stick*).
3. **Wait.** The hole is now a mound of fresh earth. A game day (24 minutes)
   after you buried it, the mound is gone: what you planted sprouts, and
   anything else is buried for good. A hole you leave empty fills itself in
   a day after it was dug.

| Plant this | Grows into | In | Then |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed Fern Seed, Seed Fern Root | A seed fern | 14 days | 1–2 seeds each harvest, growing back every 5 days (2.5 if you work compost in round it within a day of picking). Two mattock swings dig it up for 2 roots. |
| Calamites, Tree Fern, Lepidodendron or Sigillaria Root (from a dug-out stump) | That tree | 14 days | Fell it like any other. Its stump grows back, unless you dig it out. |

**Shoots.** A young tree fern, Calamites or seed fern (one you planted, or a
stump growing back) has a tender shoot while it is under a third grown.
Press E to pick it: greens to eat raw. The plant starts growing again from
its roots, and has no new shoot to pick for a game day. Lepidodendron and
Sigillaria have no shoots.

**Digging up.** Swing the mattock at a young plant to dig it up for its root
(two swings for a young tree); it is gone, and the spot is free.

## Compost

Make a **Compost Basket** at a workbench with the Woodworking Tools on its
rack: 4 boards, 6 sticks and 2 cordage. Set it down with Use, then look at it
and press E to put in what you hold. It fills to level 20:

| Put in | Counts | Composting time |
|---|---|---|
| Any food (not a drink in its pot or canteen), guts, a small carcass | 2 | 15 days |
| A grub (raw or roasted) | 1 | 15 days |
| A stick | 2 | 30 days |
| A branch | 4 | 30 days |
| A log (carry it to the basket, press E) | 10 | 60 days |

Only the slowest thing in it sets the time; they don't add up. Once it is
full it composts and takes nothing more. When it is done, press E to dig out
**5 Compost**; the basket is empty again. An empty basket can be picked back
up (E with empty hands).

Meat, fish, guts and carcasses smell. Each time you sleep, a basket with
meat in it has a 15% chance of drawing a Scorpion or an Arthropleura that
roots about beside it. It leaves the compost alone, but watch out when you
next go there.

**Using compost:**

- Plant one in a hole along with a seed or root (kneel at the hole, C).
  What sprouts grows in 7 days instead of 14, and a seed fern grown in it
  gives twice the seeds each time.
- Hold it and right-click a growing plant: a young plant, a stump growing
  back, or a picked plant growing back. It cuts 30% off the time it has left,
  and you can do it again. A planted seed fern fed within a game day of
  picking its seeds has seeds again 2.5 days after picking, not 5.

## Crafting on the ground

There is no crafting menu. Press **C** (D-pad down on a gamepad) to kneel and
work on the ground in front of you. You can't do it standing in water, while
something has hold of you, or with the inventory open.

1. **Lay out the parts.** Your inventory shows as a tray along the bottom.
   Click an item to set one down, right-click to set down the whole stack.
   Click a pile on the ground to pick one back up (right-click for all).
2. **Choose what to make.** The panel on the right lists what the laid-out
   parts are for, the ones that use the most of what you laid out first.
   Anything still missing is listed under it in grey. Point at one to read
   how it's done in the instructions box above the tray.
3. **Do the work by hand.** Each recipe has its own short action:
   - **Twist** (cordage, torches): hold the left mouse button and move the
     mouse back and forth to roll the work under your hand. You see it roll,
     hear the fibers rustle, and a bar under the instructions fills up.
   - **Steps** (hafting, campfire, pot, bandage, sack,
     crushing wax):
     click the glowing marker on the work again and again. Each click is one
     wrap, coil, fold or pound.
   - **Knap** (tool heads): strike flakes off a chert nodule. See below.
4. Press **C** again (or Done) to stand up. Anything still on the ground goes
   back into your inventory.

The view turns, widens and if need be steps back so the work and every spot
you need to click stay clear of the panels.

On a gamepad, the right stick moves a cursor drawn on the screen, A clicks
(on a tray slot, lays one down), the left trigger right-clicks (on a tray
slot, lays the whole stack), X does the next step (hold it to twist cordage)
and B stands you up. These are the default buttons; they follow whatever you
bind under Settings → Controls.

**Stop** puts the parts back down, except for a stone you have already struck,
which is lost. Getting hurt or grabbed stands you up, and saving puts
everything back in your inventory first.

## The workbench

A workbench is a plain wooden table with a tool rack along its back.

1. **Build it.** Kneel (C) on dry ground with room in front of you and
   lay out **4 boards, 4 sticks and 2 cordage**. Choose *Build a workbench*
   and click the glowing spots (8 steps): the legs go up, get lashed, the top
   boards go on and the rack board goes along the back. It stands where you
   built it, about 1.7 m in front of you, and stays there (it is saved with
   the world). The ground needn't be level: the legs reach down to it. If
   something is in the way, or the ground is wet, the option tells you what it
   needs. You step back a little while you build so the whole frame is in
   view.
2. **Work at it.** Face the bench and press C. You stand at its front and lay
   things out on its top just as on the ground. Everything you can make by
   hand can be made here too, and so can the things only a bench allows.
3. **Hang tools on the rack.** Hold a workbench tool (select it in the hotbar)
   and press E at the bench to hang it on the next free peg. There are four.
   Look at a hanging tool and press E to take it back down.
4. **Move it.** Hit the bench 3 times within 4 seconds with an axe or a
   mattock to knock it loose. Its rack tools go to your bag (or drop at your
   feet if it's full) and you pick the bench up in your arms like a log.
   Right-click (or Q) sets it up in front of you, if there's room and the
   ground is dry. You can carry it up a house's rope ladder (it rides on your
   back while you climb) and set it up inside.

Made only at a workbench, with or without tools on its rack:

| Item | Needs | Action | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unfired Platter | 4 wet clay | 5 steps | Fire it in a campfire to make a **Clay Platter**, for forming clay on |
| Woodworking Tools | 2 chert nodules, 2 sticks, 1 cordage, and a river stone to work with | 6 steps | A chert chisel and saw, for finer woodwork |
| Unfired Bowl | 3 wet clay | 4 steps | Fire it in a campfire to make a **Clay Bowl** |
| Papermaking Tools | 1 clay bowl, 4 sticks, 6 plant fiber, 1 cordage | 6 steps | The bowl and a fiber screen, for making paper from wood or plants |
| Unfired Spindle Whorl | 1 wet clay | 3 steps | Fire it in a campfire to make a **Spindle Whorl** |
| Spindle | 1 spindle whorl, 1 stick | 2 steps | Spins plant fiber into string (see [String and the bow drill](#string-and-the-bow-drill)) |

The platter, woodworking tools, papermaking tools and spindle hang on the
rack. Once they do, they unlock advanced recipes at that bench. You only learn
these once you have made or held the tool (select it in the hotbar), or used
a bench (E, or kneeling at it) with it on the rack: until then they aren't in
the recipe book and the bench doesn't offer them. Using any bench also puts
the bench recipes in your book.

### String and the bow drill

The **spindle** is the first step to a bow drill:

1. At a workbench, shape an **Unfired Spindle Whorl** from 1 wet clay and fire
   it in a campfire.
2. At a workbench, fit the **Spindle Whorl** on a stick to make a **Spindle**.
   Making it (or holding it, or using a bench with one on its rack) puts
   String and the Bow Drill in your recipe book.
3. **String**: 3 plant fiber, spun at a workbench with the spindle either on
   its rack or in your bag (Twist: drag to keep it turning). String is finer
   and stronger than cordage. It can't be made by hand on the ground.
4. **Bow Drill**: 2 sticks and 1 string, strung at a workbench (3 steps). The
   spindle doesn't have to be there. Keep the bow drill in your inventory and
   it lights any campfire with fuel.
5. **Woven Cloth Sack**: 8 string, woven by hand (8 steps). Known once you
   have a spindle. It carries 40 kg more and holds 100 kg set down.

| Item | Needs on the rack | Needs | Action | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unfired Soup Pot | Clay Platter | 10 wet clay, 3 boards | 8 steps | A big pot on a board tripod: fire it over a campfire to make a **Soup Pot** (see [Soup](#soup)) |
| Wooden Canteen | Woodworking Tools | 1 board, 2 waxy residue, 1 cordage | 6 steps | A flask for two servings of water or soup (see [Canteens](#canteens)) |
| Unfired Unsealed Fermentation Vessel | Clay Platter | 8 wet clay | 6 steps | An open crock for pickling (see [Fermentation](#fermentation)) |
| Unfired Sealed Fermentation Vessel | Clay Platter | 10 wet clay | 7 steps | A lidded jar for brewing alcohol or salting (see [Fermentation](#fermentation)) |
| Wet Sheet | Papermaking Tools | 6 plant fiber, 1 pot of swamp or boiled water | 5 steps | Dry it in a campfire to make a **Big Blank Sheet**. You keep the empty pot |
| Unfired Pencil Core | Papermaking Tools | 1 ash, 1 wet clay | 3 steps | Fire it in a campfire to make a **Pencil Core** |
| String | Spindle (or in your bag) | 3 plant fiber | Twist | Finer, stronger cord: a bow drill needs it |
| Fishing Rod Blank | Woodworking Tools | 1 branch | 4 steps | The rod of a fishing rod (see [Fishing gear](#fishing-gear)) |
| Ash Pencil | Papermaking Tools | 1 pencil core, 1 stick, 1 cordage, and any blade (chert blade or stone axe) to work with | 4 steps | Split the stick, lay the core in it, close and bind it. 50 uses |
| Softwood House Blueprint | Papermaking Tools | 1 big blank sheet, an ash pencil with 2 uses left, and any blade to sharpen it | 10 steps | Hold it to see where the house would go, right-click to mark out the site (see [The softwood house](#the-softwood-house)) |

More advanced clay and woodworking recipes are still to come.

### Fishing gear

A **Fishing Rod** is made from three parts, then put together at any
workbench (no tools on its rack needed). See [Fishing](#fishing) for using it.

| Part | Made from | Where | In the book once |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fishing Rod Blank** | 1 branch (4 steps: saw off the side shoots, scrape a taper) | A workbench with the **Woodworking Tools** on its rack | You have made or held the woodworking tools, or used a bench with them on its rack |
| **Fishing Line** | 3 string (Twist: braid them) | By hand, anywhere | You have a spindle (made, held, or on the rack of a bench you used) |
| **Chitin Hook** | 1 chitin plate, and any blade (chert blade or stone axe) to work with | Any workbench | You have a workbench |
| **Fishing Rod** | 1 rod blank, 1 fishing line, 1 chitin hook (3 steps) | Any workbench | You have a workbench |

The hook is **knapped** like a chert head, but from a chitin plate, flaked
with a blade instead of struck with a river stone. A chitin plate only ever
comes out as a chitin shape (a hook), never as a chert tool head.

**Bait**: meat bait, beetle grubs, rotten food and guts all work as bait.
Any raw meat item tears into **2 Meat Bait** by hand (3 steps): a piece of raw
meat or fish meat, gnawed meat, any dressed small prey (Hylonomus, Petrolacosaurus, Archaeothyris, Amphibamus, Tuditanus, Morganucodon), or any small fish. Small
fish and fish meat aren't bait as they are. Meat bait goes off like raw meat, and rotten food is still
bait.

### Fishing

1. **Bait the rod.** Kneel (C), on the ground or at a workbench, and lay out
   the fishing rod with some bait. Choose *Bait the rod with …* and click the
   hook. The rod's name shows its bait. Which bait you use makes no
   difference to what you catch.
2. **Cast.** Hold the rod and Use (right-click, the left trigger, or *Use* on
   a touch screen): the hook flies like a thrown stone. With no bait on the
   rod it won't cast. Use again with nothing on reels the line back in, bait
   and all.
3. **Wait.** In the water, every 5 seconds there is a 15% chance of a bite.
   A bite always takes the bait. Three bites in ten only take the bait (the
   rod tugs, then nothing): reel in and bait again.
4. **Reel it in.** When a fish is on, the rod jerks in your hand. It swims
   away from you, weaving and taking line out; each Use pulls it a stretch
   closer. If it gets too far away (30 m) it is gone, bait and all.
5. **Land it.** Once it is ashore it flops where it lies. Reeling, or walking
   away, drags it across the ground: drag it well up the bank, out of reach
   of the water, then to your feet.
6. **Strike it** with a weapon (or your fists).

| Fish | How often | Size | Gives | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haplolepis | the commonest | tiny: into the bag | Roast it whole (a mouthful), or tear it up for bait | |
| Elonichthys | common | small: into the bag | Roast it whole, or bait | |
| Amphicentrum (deep, round body) | fairly common | small: into the bag | Roast it whole, or bait | |
| Acanthodes (a "spiny shark") | fairly common | small: into the bag | Roast it whole, or bait | **Don't punch it bare-handed**: its fin spines jab you. Its carcass is safe |
| Bandringa (paddle-snouted shark) | rare | small: into the bag | Roast it whole, or bait | |
| Rhabdoderma (a coelacanth) | fairly common | big: carried | Dress it on the ground (C): 3 Fish Meat | |
| Ctenodus (a lungfish) | uncommon | big: carried | 4 Fish Meat | Breathes air: lives far longer out of the water |
| Sagenodus (a lungfish) | uncommon | big: carried | 4 Fish Meat | Breathes air, too |
| Megalichthys (enamel-scaled) | uncommon | big: carried | 6 Fish Meat | Fights hard on the line |
| Xenacanthus (freshwater shark) | rare | big: carried | 5 Fish Meat | **Snaps at you** when landed: strike it fast |
| Rhizodus | very rare | very big: carried | 10 Fish Meat | |
| Strepsodus | very rare | very big: carried | 10 Fish Meat | |
| Orthacanthus (giant shark) | very rare | the biggest: carried | 14 Fish Meat | **Bites hard** when landed, and its thrashing draws amphibians from further off |

Big fish are dressed on the ground like any carcass: gut, skin, then cut.

**Watch the water.** An Eogyrinus or Uranocentrodon near a hooked fish, in the
water or just up the bank, may snatch it off your line.

Putting the rod away lets go of the line (a fish in the water swims off). You
can't sleep with a line out. A fish left on land dies after a minute and a half
(a lungfish lasts five times as long).

### Traps

Traps catch while you sleep. Bait them like a rod: kneel at the trap (C),
lay out any bait (meat bait, beetle grubs, rotten food or guts), choose
*Bait the … with …* and click the glowing spot. Baiting a trap that is
already baited swaps the bait, giving you the old one back.

**Fish Trap.** At a workbench with the Woodworking Tools on its rack: 10
sticks and 3 cordage. It is too big for the bag: it is left beside the bench,
and E picks it up to carry in your arms. Wade into shallow water and press E
to set it down on the bottom (it won't go down where the water is too deep to
reach, over about a metre). It only catches set in the water. Each time you
sleep, a baited fish trap catches 1 to 5 small fish (Haplolepis,
Elonichthys, Amphicentrum, Acanthodes or Bandringa) and the bait is eaten.
Unbaited, it catches nothing more. Fish stay alive in it for two days; after
that they are rotten food.

**Snare.** By hand, once you have a spindle: a string and a branch. Set it
on dry ground with Use. Each time you sleep, a baited snare:

| Chance | Catches | Bait |
|---|---|---|
| 70% | A small prey animal of any kind | Eaten |
| 20% | Nothing | Still there |
| 10% | A Scorpion or an Arthropleura, alive in the noose | Eaten |

A snare holds one animal at a time. A small one stays in the noose for you.
A Scorpion or Arthropleura is still there, alive, whenever you come back. It
can't get away, but it fights back: a Scorpion lunges at you if you get
close. Kill it, and butcher the carcass where it lies. Left two days, it
dies in the noose and is rotten food (3).

**Emptying a trap.** Look at it and press E: what it caught goes into your
bag as carcasses, to roast, dress or tear up like any other you killed (or
rotten food, after two days). E on an empty trap picks it up; a snare's bait
comes back with it.

### Paper and the ash pencil

A **Big Blank Sheet** and an **Ash Pencil** are what blueprints are drawn
with. Both are made at a workbench with the papermaking tools on its rack:

1. **Paper.** Lay out 6 plant fiber and a Pot of Swamp Water or a Pot of
   Boiled Water. Mash the fiber to pulp in the bowl and lift a sheet on the
   screen (5 steps). You get a Wet Sheet and the empty pot back. Set the wet
   sheet in a lit campfire, like pottery, and take it off as a Big Blank
   Sheet. It never burns.
2. **The core.** Lay out 1 ash (scooped from a campfire with a mattock) and
   1 wet clay and roll them into a rod (3 steps). Fire the Unfired Pencil
   Core in a campfire to get a Pencil Core.
3. **The pencil.** Lay out the pencil core, a stick and a cordage, with a
   chert blade or a stone axe laid out or in your bag. Split the stick, lay
   the core in the groove, close the halves and bind them (4 steps). The
   blade is kept. A pencil has 50 uses, shown on it as it wears.

## The softwood house

The first blueprint is a **Softwood House**: a small square hut on stilts,
with a room, a front porch under the roof, two windows, a doorway, a
fireplace and a rope ladder up to the porch.

**Drawing it.** At a workbench with the papermaking tools on the rack, lay
out a Big Blank Sheet with an Ash Pencil laid out or in your bag, and a blade
(chert blade or stone axe) to sharpen it. Drawing takes 10 steps. At the
start and halfway the pencil is sharpened, and each sharpening wears one use
off it. The most worn pencil that has 2 uses left is used.

**Marking out the site.** Hold the blueprint to see the house as a blue
outline in front of you. It turns red, and says why, where it can't go:

- a stilt or the foot of the ladder would stand in water
- a tree, boulder or anything solid is in the way
- another house stands within 9 m

Right-click to mark out the site. That uses up the blueprint, and the outline
stays there until the house is finished. Three blows with an axe or a
mattock within a few seconds tear down an unfinished site. Everything
already built is dropped back on the ground as the logs, boards, cordage,
sticks, stones and clay it took. The blueprint is lost. A finished house
can't be torn down.

**Building it.** Bring each part's materials in your bag, look at the
outline and press E. You are put where that part is built, and each step
puts one thing in place: a board laid and lashed, a cord tied, a stone set,
a lump of clay pressed in. Stopping partway gives the materials back. The
parts go up in order:

| Part | Needs | Steps | Built from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 stilts, one at a time | a 2 m Calamites log (or stripped log) carried in your arms, and a river stone to pound it in (kept) | 5 each | the ground |
| Floor | 6 tree fern boards, 3 cordage | 9 | the ground |
| Porch | 4 tree fern boards, 3 cordage | 7 | the ground |
| Rope ladder | 6 sticks, 4 cordage | 10 | the ground |
| Back wall | 3 tree fern boards, 2 cordage | 5 | up in the house |
| Left wall (with a window) | 3 tree fern boards, 1 cordage | 4 | up in the house |
| Right wall (with a window) | 3 tree fern boards, 1 cordage | 4 | up in the house |
| Front wall (with the doorway) | 3 tree fern boards, 2 cordage | 5 | up in the house |
| Fireplace | 8 river stones, 6 wet clay | 14 | up in the house |
| Ceiling | 8 Calamites boards, 4 cordage | 12 | up in the house |
| Roof, left side | 4 tree fern boards, 4 cordage | 8 | the ground on that side |
| Roof, right side | 4 tree fern boards, 4 cordage | 8 | the ground on that side |

That is 4 Calamites logs, 30 tree fern boards, 8 Calamites boards, 28
cordage, 6 sticks, 8 river stones and 6 wet clay, over 100 steps. Parts
built from the ground can also be built from up in the house.

**The ladder.** Walk into it facing the house to climb (look down to climb
down), and at the top you step onto the porch. Walking back off it, or
facing away and walking, takes you down. You can climb it carrying anything:
a workbench, a soup pot or vessel, a log or a carcass rides on your back, and
you can't set it down until you're off the ladder. Set down up there, it stays
on the floor, and a pot or vessel put down by the fireplace goes on it.

**Living in it.** Once the house is finished:

- nothing spawns near it, and no animal goes for you inside or on the porch
- sleeping there is safe: no ambush, no predators, no chill, no flooding
- sacks, piles and anything else you set down stay up on the floor (but no
  campfire on the wooden floor)
- the fireplace works like a campfire, with fuel, cooking spots and ash, but
  it can't be knocked apart

## Soup

A **Soup Pot** is a big clay pot hanging from a tripod of three boards. It is
far too big for the bag: like a log, you carry it in your arms, and it keeps
whatever is in it while you do.

- **Carrying**: E picks up an empty pot; C picks up a pot with something in
  it (E is for eating). Q or E puts it down. Put it down by a campfire and it
  hangs over the middle of the fire, high enough to clear the flames, so all
  six cooking spots round the edge stay free. One pot to a fire. A house's
  fireplace works too: carry the pot up the ladder.
- **Firing**: the pot is made unfired at a workbench with the Clay Platter
  on its rack, and left standing beside the bench. Hang it over a lit fire
  for about 3½ minutes to fire it. An unfired pot won't hold water.
- **Water**: carry the fired pot to the water, look at the water and press E
  to fill it: 16 servings of swamp water. Hang it over a lit fire and it
  comes to the boil in about a minute; boiled water is clean. A Clay Pot
  held up to it (E) scoops out a Pot of Boiled Water (or swamp water, if it
  hasn't boiled). With empty hands, E drinks a serving straight from it.
- **Making soup**: once the water has boiled, hold any food and press E to
  add it. Every food counts as its cooked form: raw meat gives the soup what
  cooked meat would. The pot holds up to 12 items, and each one tops the
  water back up by 1⅓ servings, so 12 items always make a full 16-serving
  pot. Adding food takes it off the boil: let it boil again before you eat,
  or raw food in it can still make you sick.
- **Eating**: with empty hands, E eats a serving straight from the pot, with
  its share of everything in it and 30 water. Hold an empty Clay Pot and E
  scoops one serving into a **Pot of Soup** to carry in your bag; it keeps as
  long as the soup in the pot would have. Eating a serving takes its share of
  the food out too, so you can keep adding to a pot as you eat from it.
- **Looking at the pot** shows what it holds: its state (boiling, needs
  boiling, cold...), the meat, starch and greens in each serving (as a
  percentage of the bar each fills) and how many servings are left.
- **Keeping**: soup keeps for 36 game hours (a day and a half) after it last
  boiled. Leave it hanging over a fire that stays lit and it never goes off.
  Gone off, it can't be saved: boiling doesn't help, and nothing more can go
  in.
- **Emptying**: filling a pot that has something in it at the water tips it
  all out and fills it with swamp water. Tipping out soup with meat in it
  puts blood in the water (see [Creatures](creatures.md#getting-water-without-being-eaten)).

## Canteens

A **Wooden Canteen** is a hollowed length of jointed stem, sealed inside with wax, with a carved wooden lid and a cord. It holds two
servings, both of the same thing: swamp water, boiled water or soup. Empty it
weighs 1.2 kg, a fifth less than a clay pot, and each serving adds 1.5 kg, as
in a pot.

- **At the water**, E fills an empty (or half full of swamp water) canteen
  with two servings of swamp water.
- **At a soup pot**, hold it and press E to scoop a serving in: boiled water,
  swamp water or soup. Press again for the second. Swamp water and boiled
  water don't mix, nor do water and soup. Two servings of soup from
  different pots mix into one.
- **Drinking** (Eat/Drink, or right-click it in your hand) takes one serving
  and leaves the canteen half full, in the same slot. Boiled water in it
  washes wounds, a serving at a time.
- Soup in a canteen keeps as long as the soup in the pot would have (the
  older serving counts). Wood can't go on the fire, so you can't boil water
  in a canteen: boil it in a pot or a soup pot first.

## Fermentation

Two **fermentation vessels**, made at a workbench with the clay platter on
its rack. Like the soup pot they are too big for the bag: carry them (E, or
C once something is in them), and set one down by a campfire and it sits in
the middle of the fire (one pot or vessel to a fire). The first time, that
fires it.

Each holds up to **10 food**, **10 servings of water** and (sealed) **10
salt**. Nothing spoils inside.

- **Water**: hold a pot (or canteen) of boiled water and press E to pour a
  serving in, or carry the vessel to the water and fill it with swamp water
  (E while looking at the water), then set it in a lit fire to boil. Food only
  goes in once the water is clean and the vessel has cooled (a few minutes off
  the fire).
- **Adding**: hold food (or salt) and press E. **Right-click** opens the
  vessel to see what is inside and take things out, and to put in anything
  you carry.
- **Rancid**: more food than water, or (pickling and brewing) any raw meat
  or fish, or more than 30% of the food meat not yet pickled or preserved,
  and the mix goes rancid: it all rots. Carry it to the
  water and fill it to wash it out. Washing out meat puts blood in the water,
  which draws amphibians, as with the soup pot. The prompt warns you before
  you add something that would spoil it.

| Method | Vessel | Needs | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pickling (lacto fermentation) | Unsealed | Food in clean water, a game day | **Pickled** food: 10% more nourishing, plus a little greens and starch |
| Brewing (alcoholic fermentation) | Sealed, no salt | Food in clean water, a game day | Food **preserved in alcohol** (10% more nourishing, plus a little greens and starch, and it counts as a drink), and alcohol to draw off |
| Salting | Sealed, with salt | As much water and as much salt as food | **Salted** food, at once: as nourishing, but it makes you thirsty. Any amount of meat is fine. Taking food out takes a measure of salt with it |

Pickled, alcohol-preserved and salted food keeps twice as long in your bag.

**Alcohol**: once a brew has fermented a day, hold a canteen and press E to
draw off ten measures of alcohol, using a serving of the brew's water (the
bar along the bottom of its slot shows how many are left). You
can't draw so much that more food than liquid is left. Each measure can be
drunk (a little food, starch and water, and a drink's worth of alcohol) or used to
clean a wound (Treat): it fights moderate and major infections (see
[Survival](survival.md#infection)). Too many drinks are dangerous: see
[Survival](survival.md#alcohol).

## The recipe book

materials, fire, containers, medicine and gear. Recipes that need a tool on
the workbench rack are kept apart on a **Rack tools** page, grouped by tool,

## Knapping

Chert nodules flake into sharp tool heads. Lay a nodule on the ground and
choose the head you want. You need a river stone to strike with, either laid
out or in your inventory, and you keep it afterwards.

The nodule sits on a flat anvil stone with the shape you want outlined on it.
Click a part of the stone to knock a flake off it. Leave exactly the outlined
shape (or its mirror image). Knock off a part that should stay and you may end
up with a different head, or with nothing usable. If another head's shape turns
up on the way to the one you chose, you keep going: the work only ends early on
a different head once the one you chose can no longer be made.

## Fire and cooking

1. Place a Campfire (hold it and right-click).
2. Light it. Early on that means an **ember** (below). A Bow Drill in your
   inventory, or a lit torch in your hand, also lights it: look at the fire
   and press E.
3. Hold sticks, fronds or tinder and press E at the fire to add fuel. A stick
   burns for over 2.5 minutes, a frond 40 seconds and tinder about half a
   minute. A fire holds up to 40 minutes of fuel. Rain makes fuel burn almost
   twice as fast.
4. Hold raw food, an Unfired Pot or a Pot of Swamp Water and press E to set it
   at the edge of the fire. There is room for six things at once, and you can
   see each one sitting by the flames.
5. Look at a cooking item to see how far along it is, and press E to **take it
   off**. Nothing comes off by itself: you are told when something is ready.

| On the fire | Ready after | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Fiddlehead | 20 s | Cooked Fiddlehead |
| Seed | 24 s | Roasted Seed |
| Grub | 16 s | Roasted Grub |
| Gnawed Meat | 24 s | Cooked Scraps |
| Raw Meat | 40 s | Cooked Meat |
| Dressed Hylonomus | 36 s | Roast Hylonomus |
| Dressed Petrolacosaurus | 32 s | Roast Petrolacosaurus |
| Dressed Archaeothyris | 48 s | Roast Archaeothyris |
| Dressed Amphibamus | 24 s | Roast Amphibamus |
| Dressed Tuditanus | 32 s | Roast Tuditanus |
| Dressed Morganucodon | 20 s | Roast Morganucodon |
| Meganeura Carcass | 24 s | Roast Meganeura |
| Pot of Swamp Water | 48 s | Pot of Boiled Water |
| Unfired Pot | 100 s | Clay Pot |

**Food burns.** Leave it on for four times its cooking time and it turns into
Charred Remains, which are barely worth eating (they still burn as fuel). Pots
never burn.

Predators keep away from a lit fire, and a fire makes a sleeping spot much
safer. The fire is a small one: dim, quiet, and lighting only a few metres
around it.

### Coals and embers

When a fire runs out of fuel it doesn't go dark at once: it **glows red with
coals** for three times as long as it burned (a fire that burned for ten
minutes glows for thirty). Coals are too low to cook on, and they die down
faster in the rain.

- **Relight it**: add fuel to the coals (E with sticks, fronds or tinder),
  then press E to **blow on them**. It burns again.
- **Take an ember**: with no fuel in the fire, press E at the coals. You can
  also take embers from the stump the lightning set alight when you arrived,
  while it burns (a day) or glows with cinders (three days after that). A
  lit torch can also be lit at the burning stump.

An **Ember** goes out after **three game hours** (three minutes), whether it
is in your bag, a stash or on the ground. Its freshness bar shows how long it
has left. To light a fire with it:

1. Put fuel in an unlit campfire.
2. Hold the ember and press E: it is set in the tinder, keeping the time it
   had left.
3. Press E again to **blow on it**, and the fire catches.

Embers are how you get fire early on. Later, a **Bow Drill** (2 sticks and a
string, made at a workbench once you have a spindle: see
[String and the bow drill](#string-and-the-bow-drill)) lights a fire
anywhere, any time.

### Torches

Crush a frond with a river stone to get **Waxy Residue**, then roll a stick in
two of them to make a **Torch**. Hold the torch and press E at a lit fire to
light it. A lit torch lights the way around you, burns for 14 minutes (twice
as fast in the rain, when it also gives very little light), and can light an
unlit campfire. When it burns out you are left with a **Charred Stick**, which
burns well as fuel or can be rolled in wax again for a new torch.

### The mattock

A **Stone Mattock** works campfires:

- **Swing it** (left mouse) at a campfire to knock it apart. You get its two
  river stones back, plus any ash in it. Whatever was cooking falls to the
  ground.
- **Press E** at a campfire with it in hand to scoop out the ash: one Ash for
  every 200 seconds the fire has burned. Ash has no use yet.

## Weapons and striking

Left mouse attacks with what you hold. Bare hands do 2 damage, a river stone
4, a chert blade 7, a stone axe 12 and a chert spear 16. Longer weapons reach
further.

Hold a River Stone or Chert Nodule and right-click to **throw** it. See
[Creatures](creatures.md) for what a thrown stone does to each animal.
