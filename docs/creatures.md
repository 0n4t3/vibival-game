# Creatures

[Guide home](README.md)

The swamp is alive day and night. Some animals are food, some will hurt you if
you corner them, and the big amphibians are the main threat in the game.
Everything you kill leaves a carcass you can butcher for meat (see
[Butchering](#butchering)).

The animals fall into groups. Animals in the same group act much the same way.

| Group | Animals | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| [Large creatures](#large-creatures) | Uranocentrodon, Eogyrinus | The main threat. They live along the water and grab you. |
| [Hostile medium creatures](#hostile-medium-creatures) | Pulmonoscorpius | Come for you on sight. |
| [Neutral medium creatures](#neutral-medium-creatures) | Arthropleura | Leave you alone unless you attack them. |
| [Predator insects](#predator-insects) | Meganeura | Small nips. They hunt the small animals. |
| [Prey](#prey) | Hylonomus, Petrolacosaurus, Archaeothyris, Amphibamus, Tuditanus, Morganucodon | Harmless. They run from you and are food for everything else. |

## Large creatures

### Eogyrinus

A large carnivorous amphibian, about 4–5 m long.

- Turns up often, and up to four can be about at once. Usually it is on
  the bank near water, sometimes in the water, and rarely further inland,
  mostly by puddles and small ponds. A submerged one stirs the water with
  constant ripples.
- It charges anyone who comes close and seizes them in its jaws. **Stab fast**
  (left mouse) to break its grip; the grip bar shows how close you are. A
  better weapon breaks the grip faster.
- Arriving in a Uranocentrodon's water, it is sometimes set upon and either
  eaten or chased out onto land.
- Its carcass is dragged, and gives 8 raw meat.

### Uranocentrodon

A colossal flat-headed amphibian, about 7 m long, and the reason to fear the
water. (It actually lived in the Triassic, so it is out of its time here.)

- **Basking**: by day several lie out on the banks. If you come within about
  30 m, a basking one creeps toward you, low and slow, until you are in
  reach. It won't follow you far from the water.
- **Buried**: many more lie buried in the mud under the shallows, day and
  night, out of sight. The only sign is a small, faint ring of ripples every
  few seconds where it breathes.
- A buried one strikes from as far as about 16 m away the moment you come
  near the water's edge: within about twice the distance from which you could
  drink or fill a pot. It takes animals at the edge too, and hauls its catch
  out into deep water fast. It kills faster than an Eogyrinus.
- **Escaping**: once it has you in deep water (or after a moment of thrashing)
  it either lets go or it does not. The odds are 10%, raised by the best
  strike you landed while held: +15% bare-handed, +30% with a stone weapon
  (+60% with an advanced weapon, not in the game yet). The odds show on screen
  while you are held. If it lets go, you are out in deep water where others
  may lie: swim for the bank.
- Its carcass is dragged, and gives 14 raw meat.

**Bait box.** A [bait box](crafting.md#the-bait-box) thrown into the water
draws the Eogyrinus and Uranocentrodon round it to tear at it for half a
minute, coming only through the water: a way to clear a stretch of bank.

### Getting water without being eaten

Uranocentrodon and Eogyrinus are common along every stretch of shore. Most
deaths happen at the water's edge.

1. **Watch the water** for a few seconds before you approach. Faint, small
   rings mean a buried Uranocentrodon; steady ripples mean an Eogyrinus.
2. **Throw a stone in first.** A splash draws any amphibian within about 12 m
   that is not already after you. It erupts at the splash, snaps at it and
   settles there, away from you. Throw it a little way off to one side of
   where you want to drink.
3. **Pick your spot.** Rocky banks and high ground are safer than muddy flats.
   A buried Uranocentrodon will not climb up onto high ground after you.
4. **Fill a pot, don't sip.** A fired Clay Pot holds 35–40 water in one trip.
   Boil it at camp.
5. **Don't stay.** Drink or fill, then step back from the edge. Anything
   buried within about 16 m comes for you as soon as you are that close.
6. **Never sleep near the water.** Within 10 m of swamp water a night attack
   is almost certain.
7. **Don't tip meat into the water.** Filling a soup pot that still has soup
   with meat in it puts blood in the water. Every Uranocentrodon and Eogyrinus
   lurking within about 45 m that is too far off to strike comes up to the
   surface and swims slowly in toward you, for up to two minutes. Once it is
   close enough it strikes as it would from hiding. Get away from the edge.
   Empty a meaty pot by eating it, or fill it somewhere you can retreat from.

## Hostile medium creatures

### Pulmonoscorpius

A giant scorpion, out only at night and after your first few minutes in the
swamp.

- A hunter: it comes for you when it notices you.
- Its sting can inject venom. One sting stops you sprinting until it wears
  off (rest helps); three in quick succession kill.
- It fears fire. A lit campfire keeps scorpions away from your camp.
- It will not follow you into deeper water.
- It hunts small prey too, when you are not nearby. If you walk right up to a
  feeding scorpion, it leaves its meal and goes for you.
- Its carcass is carried, and gives 2 raw meat and a chitin plate.

## Neutral medium creatures

### Arthropleura

A millipede-like arthropod over two metres long. Slow, armoured and very
tough.

- Leaves you alone unless you attack it. Hit it and it turns on you with a
  heavy bite for a while.
- Preys on small prey: it chases down any that come within a few metres,
  kills them, and eats the whole body in about 5 seconds.
- Its carcass is carried, and gives 5 raw meat and 4 chitin plates.

## Predator insects

### Meganeura

A giant dragonfly with a wingspan of about 70 cm.

- Circles over its patch of swamp, now and then darting at you for a small
  bite. It keeps away from fire.
- Hunts small prey: it dives on one out in the open and carries it off.
- A thrown stone knocks it out of the air for a few seconds. If it was
  carrying an animal, it drops it.
- Its carcass goes into your bag and is roasted whole.

## Prey

Six kinds of small animal, all harmless, and all treated the same way:

- They flee from you when you come close.
- They are food for other animals. **Meganeura swoop down, seize one in their
  legs and fly off with it**, then eat it somewhere else. **Arthropleura and
  Pulmonoscorpius run them down** when they stray near, and eat the whole
  body in about 5 seconds.
- A thrown stone dazes one for a few seconds, long enough to walk up and
  kill it.
- The carcass goes into your bag. Gut and skin it by hand (no tools, and it
  leaves a small guts pile), then roast it whole on a fire.

They share one population: **up to 4 are about by day and 2 at night**, of
whatever kinds. More kinds means more variety, not more animals.

| Animal | What it is | Look for | Out | Food |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hylonomus | A small early reptile, lizard-sized | Brown, the plain one | The commonest by day | Roast: a little less than cooked meat |
| Petrolacosaurus | A slender early reptile | Sandy, a dark stripe down the back, long legs and a very long tail | Mostly by day | About the same as a Hylonomus. It spots you from further off and runs faster: throw a stone |
| Archaeothyris | A heavy-headed early relative of mammals | Dark brown with a pale belly, a deep head and jaw, the biggest | Mostly by day | The best of them: more than cooked meat. Slower, but tougher |
| Amphibamus | A little frog-headed amphibian | Olive with dark spots, a broad flat head, big eyes, a stub of a tail | Day and night, **on the banks by the water** | Small, about half a Hylonomus. Hunting it means going near the water |
| Tuditanus | A short-bodied amphibian | Reddish grey with pale bands, a short snout | **Mostly at night** | A little less than a Hylonomus |
| Morganucodon | A tiny early mammal, like a shrew (it lived much later, so it is out of its time here) | Furry, grey-brown, a pointed snout, round ears and a thin tail; the smallest | Day and night, more at night | The smallest meal, under half a Hylonomus |

## Butchering

A kill leaves a **carcass**. What you do with it depends on its size.

| Size | Animals | Moving it | Preparing it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | The six small prey, Meganeura | E puts it in your bag | Small prey: gut and skin by hand (C), then roast. Dragonfly: roast whole |
| Medium | Pulmonoscorpius, Arthropleura | E picks it up and you carry it | Butcher where it lies, with a blade |
| Large | Eogyrinus, Uranocentrodon | E and you drag it, at half walking speed | Butcher where it lies, with a blade |

**Carrying and dragging.** The carcass is in front of you, so your hands are
full. You can't sprint, use or pick up items, open your bag, craft or sleep
until you put it down with **Q** or **E**. A carcass doesn't count toward
your carry weight. You drop it if something grabs you.

**Butchering where it lies.** Look at a medium or large carcass and press
**C** to kneel beside it. It is done in three stages, one after another, and
every stage needs a blade (a Chert Blade or a Stone Axe) in your bag or laid
out:

1. **Gut it.** The guts are left in a pile beside the carcass.
2. **Skin it.** A scorpion's shell comes off as 1 chitin plate, an
   Arthropleura's as 4. An amphibian's skin isn't worth keeping and goes on
   the guts pile.
3. **Cut it into meat.** Each cut gives a piece of raw meat straight away, so
   you can stop part-way and come back to it.

Kneeling at a small kill lays it out in front of you instead, ready to gut.
Small prey are gutted and skinned by hand, with no tools, and that leaves a
small guts pile too.

**Carcasses rot.** One left on the ground lasts a day, and is still there
after you save and load. Scavengers will eat it. A carcass in your bag rots
like raw meat.

### Guts

Guts piles lie for a day, are saved with the world, and draw scavengers. While
you are within 160 m, every 5 minutes each pile has:

- a 25% chance of drawing an **Eogyrinus**, and
- a 25% chance of drawing a **Pulmonoscorpius or an Arthropleura** (only
  Arthropleura by day).

The animal turns up 30 to 50 m away and comes hungry. If you are near it or
the pile, it attacks you, even an Arthropleura. Otherwise it eats the guts and
hangs about there for 3 minutes, and attacks anyone who comes close. An
Arthropleura or scorpion that wanders past a guts pile or a carcass eats it
too.

Press **E** at a pile to pick up a handful of **Guts**. Hold them and
right-click to throw them. On land they make a new pile where they land (so do
guts you drop with Q). In the water they splash like a stone and draw any
amphibian lurking nearby, and are gone. Guts rot like raw meat.

## Stealing a kill

While an Arthropleura or scorpion is eating a small animal, you can **snatch** the
carcass (E). You get **Gnawed Meat**, which must be cooked and is worth much
less than fresh meat, and the animal turns on you at once. Snatch a medium
carcass it is eating and you carry it off, but half its meat is gone. Once
it has finished, nothing is left.

## Thrown stones

Hold a River Stone or Chert Nodule and right-click to throw it.

| Target | Effect |
|---|---|
| Small prey, Meganeura | Dazed for about 2.5 s; a Meganeura drops whatever it carries |
| Water | A splash that draws nearby amphibians to the spot |
| A big amphibian, struck directly | It comes for you |
| A tree or boulder | The stone stops and drops beside it |

Thrown stones land where they stop and can be picked up again.
